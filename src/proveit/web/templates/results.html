What this test can tell us

One project. The current implementation supports curl. Results do not establish how well it works on other repositories.

Reading code is not running it. These results cover reference checks and model analysis. Optional offline sandbox reproduction is implemented separately and is shown in a saved example; it is not included in these scores.

Uncertainty is common. 32 of 59 real reports needed human review or more information. Three more needed a policy decision.

Dataset, method & cost

The full collection contains 49 reports labeled AI slop by curl's maintainer and 148 HackerOne reports linked to curl CVEs. Reports were split by a hash of their IDs: 120 for development and 77 for the held-out test.

Rules were developed on the development split, then evaluated once on the test split. Checkers and models receive only the report's title, body, date and attachment names. Triage discussion, labels and CVE mappings are withheld.

The test cost $1.34 in model calls, about $0.017 per report. This excludes optional sandbox reproduction and external documentation search.

Reference checks alone flagged 2 of 18 slop reports and none of the 59 real reports. Many misleading reports cite real code; the harder question is whether their claims match its behavior.
